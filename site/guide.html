How this works

Waterline answers one question about a Korean rental building: is it carrying more tenant deposits than it could safely repay? It answers safe, caution or danger — and it does that without telling you, or anyone else, how much money any tenant handed over.

This page walks through what you can do here, what is genuinely running and what is only a demonstration, and what you get to keep at the end. No technical background needed.

The problem, in plain words

Under jeonse, Korea's most common way of renting a home, you do not pay monthly rent. You hand the landlord one very large lump sum — often most of what the home is worth — and you are supposed to get all of it back when you leave.

That money is only as safe as the building itself. If the landlord has taken similar deposits from several other households in the same building, and those add up to more than the building is worth, there is simply not enough to repay everyone. Whoever signed first is repaid first. Whoever signed last can be left with nothing.

Here is the trap. In one common type of Korean building, every household sits under a single ownership record. Someone about to sign cannot see how many other tenants are already ahead of them, or how much those tenants put in. You are asked to hand over your savings without being allowed to see the one figure that would tell you whether it is safe.

Waterline gives you that answer as a single word, without revealing anybody's money.

The idea, without any maths

Picture a tally stick. For centuries a debt was recorded by carving notches into a stick, then splitting the stick down its length so each side kept one half. Neither side could quietly alter their half afterwards, because it still had to line up with the other one.

Waterline works the same way. The running total of deposits is sealed into a public record that everyone can see but nobody can read. Each time a new tenant is added, the registry has to match the existing half before it is allowed to cut a new one.

So a registry that wanted to understate the total would have had to understate it every single time, going back to the very first tenant — and each of those tenants already holds a matching half. The total stays secret, and it still cannot be faked.

All you are ever told is which side of a line the hidden total falls on.

Five things you can do right now

Roughly two minutes, start to finish.

  1. Open “Check a building”

    Do this: click Check a building in the menu at the top.

    What you are looking at: one large word — the verdict for that building. Underneath it sits what the building was valued at, and the limit it is being measured against.

    Next to Senior total you will see the words not disclosed. That is not a placeholder or a loading state. That figure is genuinely unavailable — to the page, to you, and to anyone reading the public record.

  2. Watch the page check itself

    Do this: look for the line that reads “confirmed against the live ledger at block …”.

    What you are looking at: the most important thing on the site. We could have simply shown you a picture of a verdict and hoped you took our word for it. Instead, every time the page loads it goes and asks the public record directly, pulls back the raw entry for that building, and checks that what it is showing you is the same thing the record actually holds.

    If the two ever disagree, the line turns amber and says so, rather than quietly showing you something stale.

    Why it matters: you are not being asked to trust us. You are trusting a public record we do not control, and the page is showing its working in front of you. The block number is like a page number in that record — it tells you how recent the reading is.

  3. Read the privacy receipt

    Do this: find the two-column panel headed Privacy receipt.

    What you are looking at: on one side, everything the check told you. On the other, everything it deliberately did not — the individual deposits, the total, how many other households there are, and the secret used to seal the record.

    That second column is the whole point. Most safety checks work by gathering everybody's private details into one big pile and then answering questions about the pile. This one answers the question without anyone ever building the pile.

  4. Save your verification

    Do this: press Save this verification.

    What you get: one file that opens in any browser, works with no internet connection, and prints on a single sheet. It records the verdict, the building reference, the valuation, the limit, the sealed record's fingerprint, the block number, the time you took the reading, and which network it came from. Where a deposit total would normally sit, it says not disclosed.

    It also carries a QR code, and this is the part worth understanding. The document itself proves nothing. Anyone could open that file and type a different word into it. Scanning the code takes you back to the live page, which goes and checks the public record again, there and then. The sheet is a pointer to the truth rather than the truth itself — and the small print on the document says exactly that.

  5. See why a landlord cannot lie

    Do this: open Why lying fails, drag the slider to any figure you like, and press Generate certificate.

    What you are looking at: a demonstration, and we would rather say so plainly than let you assume otherwise. That page runs on a timer, is not connected to anything, and the building on it is invented. It is labelled as a simulation at the top and at the bottom.

    Why it is not live: the real calculation needs about 11 MB of cryptographic keys and several seconds of work. Putting that inside a web page would mean a slow download and a long wait on a phone, at the exact moment somebody is deciding whether to sign. We chose to keep the page instant and to be straightforward about where the demonstration ends.

    The refusal it shows you is real behaviour, and anyone can run it for themselves. That is the last section on this page.

What you can and cannot do here

You can
  • Look up a building and see its verdict.
  • Watch the page confirm that verdict against the live public record.
  • See exactly what was revealed to you and what was not.
  • Download and print a verification document.
  • Scan its code later to re-check whether anything has changed.
  • Open the real contract in an independent public explorer run by other people, not by us.
You cannot
  • Add a building, register a tenant, or issue a verdict. There is no way to write anything to the record from this website at all.
  • See any individual deposit, the total, or how many households there are. This is not hidden behind a login — it is simply not available to anyone.
  • Change a verdict that has already been published.
  • Be asked to install anything, sign up, or pay. You never connect a wallet, there is no seed phrase to lose, and nothing here costs money.

If you want to check the maths yourself

You do not need any of this to use the site. It is here for anyone who wants to confirm that the refusal on the demonstration page is genuine behaviour rather than a story we drew.

Three commands, on a Mac, a Linux machine, or Windows with WSL:

npm install compact compile +0.31.1 contracts/waterline.compact build/waterline npm run verify:refusal

The last command builds a building with two households whose deposits come to ₩5.5억, confirms that against a ₩6.0억 valuation the honest answer is danger, and then attempts the exact lie that would turn it into safe. The calculation refuses to run, and the refusal it gives is the same wording the demonstration page shows you.

Note where that refusal happens: on the landlord's own machine, before anything is sent anywhere. Nothing is submitted and nothing is rejected, because no valid proof can be built in the first place.
