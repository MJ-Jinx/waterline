Is your deposit above the line?

Find out whether the deposits already held against a building — the ones repaid before yours — add up to more than it can cover, without anyone revealing a single amount.

Under jeonse, Korea's usual way of renting, a tenant hands over a lump sum worth most of the home instead of paying monthly rent, and is repaid at the end. That money is only as safe as the building — and the other deposits loaded onto it are invisible. Waterline shows one thing: whether the building is loaded past its line.

New here? How it works explains the whole thing in plain language, including which parts are live and which are a demonstration.

The waterline is drawn as a band, never a single level —
because the page genuinely does not know the real total.

1

The total is sealed, not published

The registry seals the running total into a short code on the public record. The code gives nothing away, and the amounts themselves never go on it.

2

The answer is worked out behind the seal

The total is compared against what the building is worth, inside a proof. Only someone who can unseal the real figure can produce one, so the answer cannot be invented.

3

You see one word, and nothing else

The answer is published for anyone to read. The figures behind it stay with the registry.
