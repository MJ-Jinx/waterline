Watch the whole thing, start to finish

Press the button and this page walks through every step a registry takes: opening a building, adding each tenant's deposit to a sealed total, and working out whether the building is safe — all without the total ever being written down in the open. After each step it tells you what is coming next.

    Now try to cheat

    Put in a deposit total the registry might wish were true — something small enough to make a crowded building look safe — and press the button. The calculation runs in this tab with the false figure in place.

    The real books say ₩5.0억 across two households. Any other figure you enter is a lie about what is already sealed, and you can watch what happens to it.

    What is real here, precisely

    Real: the contract, the calculation, the sealing, and the zero-knowledge proofs — computed in this tab by the same code the registry runs, using the same proving keys. The refusal when you enter a false total is the contract's own, not a message we wrote.

    Invented: the building and its deposits. This tab makes them up, because in this demo your browser is playing the registry — the only party allowed to hold those figures. A tenant's browser could never do this, and that is the point rather than a limitation: if it could, the tenant would be holding the landlord's private books.

    Not done here: sending the result to the network. That needs a funded wallet, and putting one in a public web page would mean publishing its key. The three buildings on Check a building were submitted the proper way, from the registry's own machine, and you can read them off the chain.
